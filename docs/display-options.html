Simple or Detailed — You Choose

The same Tidesy and the same station, with the optional data layers switched off and switched on

Tidesy always shows the tide. Everything else — rain, daylight, moon, waves, water temperature, UV, air quality, storm surge and alert timing — is a layer you can switch on or off in Edit Settings (on TidesyS, the 2.8-inch Tidesy, they appear in the web dashboard only — its screen always shows the simple view). Leave them off for a clean screen you can read from across the room, or turn them on to see everything at once.

The screenshots below are from a 7-inch Tidesy showing Los Angeles on October 6, 2026, a day with three active weather alerts. Each pair was taken four minutes apart, so the tide itself has barely moved.

What the detailed screen adds

1
Ocean and air readings. Water temperature, wave height, UV index and air quality join temperature and wind in the status line. Water temperature and wave height are repeated in the left column. Environment › Ocean & Air Conditions
2
Alert bars. One bar for each active weather alert, drawn across the hours it is in effect and labelled with its name and severity. Here: a Coastal Flood Advisory and a Beach Hazards Statement until mid-afternoon, and an Extreme Heat Warning all day. Alerts › Alert Graph Overlay
3
Observed water level. Each dot is what the NOAA gauge actually measured that hour, with the difference from the prediction next to it (+1.1 means 1.1 ft higher than predicted). The dashed line carries that difference forward as a forecast for the rest of the day. Environment › Flood Risk
4
Taller scale. The graph’s scale grows to fit the forecast line, which here brings your own warning level (the red dashed line) into view. Environment › Flood Risk
5
Moon phase. Tonight’s moon and how much of it is lit. Environment › Daylight & Moon
6
Surge. How far the water is running above or below the prediction right now. Environment › Flood Risk
7
Sun and moon on the time axis. Markers for sunrise, sunset, moonrise and moonset, and a daylight strip under the graph showing night, twilight and day. Environment › Daylight & Moon
8
Rain strip. The rain forecast, hour by hour. Environment › Rain Forecast
9
Wave and UV strips. Forecast wave height in feet and the UV index for each hour, colour-coded. Environment › Ocean & Air Conditions
Flood line. When NOAA’s minor flood level for the station falls within the graph, Flood Risk also draws it as an orange dashed line labelled FLOOD LINE. At Los Angeles that level is above the top of this graph, so it is not drawn here.

What Stays the Same

These are on the screen whichever way you set the switches above:

  • The tide curve, today’s highs and lows, and the current level with its rate of change
  • Your warning level (red) and preferred tide range (orange)
  • Air temperature and wind
  • The alert banner at the top right
About the alert banner. The banner has its own switch, Alerts › Show Alerts, which was left on for both sets of screenshots. Turn it off and Tidesy shows no weather alerts at all — no banner, no alert pages and no alert bars.
To change a setting: open your Tidesy’s dashboard in a browser, choose Edit Settings, flip the switch and press Save & Restart. Tidesy restarts and comes back with the new layout in about a minute.
